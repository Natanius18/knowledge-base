# ROI and Risk Concepts

## ROI — Return on Investment

ROI measures whether an investment is worth the cost.

**Basic formula:**

ROI = (Gain from Investment - Cost of Investment) / Cost of Investment × 100%

### In software projects, consider:
- **Costs:** development time, infrastructure, maintenance, licenses.
- **Benefits:** increased revenue, reduced costs, saved time, improved productivity, better customer experience.
- **Payback:** how quickly the investment generates enough value to cover its cost.

### Example

A feature costs €10,000 to develop but is expected to save €20,000 per year.

ROI = (€20,000 - €10,000) / €10,000 = **100%**

A higher ROI generally means a more attractive investment.

## Risk

Risk is the possibility that an uncertain event will negatively affect a project or investment.

### Common types of risk:
- **Technical:** technology may not work as expected.
- **Financial:** costs may exceed the budget.
- **Schedule:** delivery may take longer than planned.
- **Business:** the solution may not provide the expected value.
- **Operational:** the system may cause problems in production.
- **Security:** vulnerabilities may lead to incidents or data loss.

### Assessing risk

A simple approach is:

**Risk = Probability × Impact**

For example:

- Probability: 20%
- Potential impact: €50,000
- Expected risk exposure: 0.2 × €50,000 = **€10,000**

## ROI vs. Risk

When evaluating an option, don't look only at ROI.

A solution with **high ROI but high risk** may be less attractive than a solution with **moderate ROI and low risk**.

A good decision considers:

**Expected value + ROI + Risk + Time + Strategic importance**

The goal is not always to maximize ROI, but to choose the option with the best overall business value for an acceptable level of risk.
